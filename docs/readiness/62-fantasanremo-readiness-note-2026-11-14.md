# 62 — FantaSanremo: first-client readiness note for the call and the Workspace demonstration (14 Nov 2026)

**Status:** preparation only. I have **not** seen the proposal the CEO sent or their messages, nothing is signed, and **no price, date, capacity, integration or service level may be promised** before verification. Items marked *(assumption)* come from public information and must be confirmed with them.

## 1. What they asked for (as reported by the CEO)
More information on our **processes, tools, possible integrations, tasks and costs**. The CEO replied with an **indicative commercial proposal**, described the "Zendesk-like" Workspace and invited them to a call.

## 2. Context to confirm on the call *(assumptions from public sources)*
FantaSanremo is a popular Italian fantasy game built around the **Sanremo music festival** (held in February). Public sources describe **very large and strongly seasonal** participation (millions of teams; hundreds of thousands of teams within a day of registration opening in 2024) and report that **the app was under strain in February 2026**, with a deadline postponed ([Lycamobile blog](https://www.lycamobile.it/blog/en/fantasanremo-digital-festival-game-2026/), [Gambling.com Italia](https://www.gambling.com/it/news/come-funziona-fantasanremo-3852200), [NSS Magazine](https://www.nssmag.com/en/lifestyle/32112/fantasanremo-origins-papalina-baudi)). **If that is the right company, the key questions are about peaks, not averages:** how many contacts a day in the weeks before and during the festival and in the rest of the year; which channels (in-app chat, email, social, store reviews); what the contacts are about (registration, payments or prizes, leagues, rules, bugs, complaints); whether **moderation** of league names, chats or user content is needed; the languages (Italian first); the hours (the festival evenings and nights). **Italian-speaking operators are the core requirement**: our recruitment strength is Italy (CEO), but **no Italian-speaking operator team exists today**, and staffing for a February peak is a hiring and training project that must be planned months ahead.

## 3. What we can honestly show and say now
| Their likely need | What the Workspace does | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Conversations in, queued and assigned | Queues, automatic assignment, operator capacity, language routing, response timers, automatic reassignment, supervisor alerts | **Live in production** (demo on the release candidate) |

| Integration | Signed webhook both ways, tested only against a simulator; a connector for their own system (or their helpdesk) is built per client | Webhook **live**; **no real client connected**; connectors **planned per client** |
| Billing | Per message, per ticket, per moderated message, hourly, hourly plus tickets | **Built** (not in production); verified-hours billing **planned** |

**Honest positioning of "Zendesk-like":** the Workspace is a **conversation-operations platform** (queues, assignment, supervision, QA, escalation, billing and reporting), **not a full helpdesk product**. Against a typical helpdesk it **does not have**: tags and custom ticket fields, saved views, merging, macros beyond quick replies, **attachments and images (the interface is text only)**, an **email channel**, satisfaction surveys, a public help centre, multi-brand setups. Whether any of these is needed depends on their channels and process; **the call should find out before we say "yes"** and before any build (the CEO's rule: no client-specific features without real requirements). Their own helpdesk, if they have one, could remain the system of record, with operators working in it (Option A) or the Workspace used for the conversation layer (Option B).

## 4. Demonstration plan (reliable, honest, about 15 minutes)
Use the **clean demo copy** with fictional data and AI off, rehearsed with the CEO beforehand (reset data; health shows AI disabled; each role in its own window). Show, in this order: (1) an Italian message arriving through the signed interface; (2) the queue and automatic assignment, response timer, language routing (an Italian operator in the demo data); (3) the operator replying and resolving; (4) the supervisor view: queue, coverage, **QA review, escalation**; (5) client reporting and usage; (6) **moderation** (clearly labelled "built, not yet in production") if they raise it. **Say, in this order:** what is live, what is built but not released, what is planned. **Do not show** billing, earnings, margins, the sales pipeline or any real credential. **Do not promise** integrations, capacity, languages staffed, dates, response times or prices.

## 5. Questions to ask on the call
Volumes by hour and by season; channels and tools (their current helpdesk or support software and whether it has an API); what the tasks are (answering, triage, refunds or prize questions, moderation); languages and hours; who the escalation contacts are; quality expectations and reporting; data they would expose to operators (personal data, payments) and their data-protection terms; start date and any pilot idea; budget logic (per ticket, per message, hourly). Ask for API documentation and a sandbox; we answer "simple, moderate or complex" before committing.

## 6. What must be true before a first project with them
The security-critical release and backups proven; 2FA enforced; alerts and monitoring live; the coverage drill done; a **named Italian-speaking team** recruited, trained and supervised (with backups for every shift); a **load and peak test** at their expected peak (we only have a laptop figure of 1,000 messages a minute for the platform; the **human** limit is what binds, see `54` section 6); their integration feasible and verified in a sandbox; the data-processing agreement and pilot terms signed after the lawyer's review; the pricing agreed in writing. **No promise on any of these until each is true.**

## 7. Risks to name internally
Seasonal spikes beyond what a small team can answer (queues grow even if the platform is fine); hiring and training lead time for Italian operators; text-only interface if they need images or attachments; the lack of any real client integration so far; a very large user base means abuse and complaint handling, not only questions; reputational risk if a pilot is accepted without staffing. Preferred response: a **small, clearly scoped pilot**, sized to staffing we actually have, with agreed limits.
