# 63 — Image sourcing and licensing for the website: options and rules (14 Nov 2026)

**Status:** proposal. **No image was downloaded, licensed or bought.** The visual layout and photo slots are in `deliverables/gco-homepage-visual-proposal-2026-11-14.html`. The CEO's rules: genuine photographs of GCO people (with consent) preferred; licensed illustrative stock acceptable at first, always labelled; never present stock models as GCO employees or a stock office as ours; no purchase without his approval.

## 1. Options
| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Real photographs of GCO people** (Founder, CTO, later operators and supervisors), a short photo session with signed consent | Authentic, no licence risk, strongest credibility | Needs willing people, a decent camera or a photographer, a suitable background | A photographer would need a quote (**not obtained**); a phone-shot session is free but quality-limited |
| **B. Free stock libraries** (for example Unsplash, Pexels) | No cost; commercial use is generally allowed without permission or attribution | **Recognisable people:** the platforms do not guarantee model releases, and Pexels' own terms say commercial use of content with people may need consent or a licence, with the responsibility on the user. Risky for a hero image of faces | Free |
| **C. Paid stock with model releases** (a subscription or single licences from a mainstream paid stock agency) | Releases and indemnity terms are part of the licence; far larger choice of "operator with headset" scenes | Cost; licence tiers (web use, size, duration) must be read | **Unknown until a quote: the CEO's approval needed before any purchase** |
| **D. Illustrations and real Workspace screenshots only** | No people issues | Does not meet the CEO's goal of "more human" | Free |
| **Not acceptable** | AI-generated faces standing in for a team; photos of other companies' offices implied as ours; stock people with names or invented quotes | Deceptive | |

## 2. Recommendation
Use **A for portraits and the About page** (the Founder and CTO, with consent) and **C for the hero, supervisor and team scenes** until real operator photographs exist, because those pictures show recognisable people and carry the most visual weight; **B is acceptable for non-people or anonymous images** (hands, desks, screens without faces) if each licence page is saved. A single hero image and a handful of supporting images would be a **small, bounded purchase**; the price must be quoted and approved first.

## 3. Rules for every image
1. Record, in a licence log: source, link, image id, photographer, date, licence type, whether a **model release** exists, the person who approved it. Keep a copy of the licence text or receipt.
2. No visible brand logos, trademarks, real client screens or readable personal data.
3. Caption stock photographs **"Illustrative image"**; never say or imply the people are GCO staff or the room is a GCO office.
4. Descriptive alternative text for every image; modern formats and responsive sizes; the hero image under about 200 KB after optimisation so pages stay fast.
5. Photographs of real people only with **signed consent** specifying where and how long the image may be used and how to withdraw it.
6. Diversity and setting should look natural and international, not staged.

## 4. Search briefs (for whoever sources the images)
Hero: "customer support agent headset laptop modern office, natural light, three-quarter view, space on the left for text". Supervisor: "team lead monitoring dashboards behind operators". Team: "small diverse team reviewing a screen together, casual professional". Training: "coach and two trainees at a desk with laptops". Per service and per industry: the scene from the layout; avoid call-centre cliches (rows of identical cubicles) and avoid anything that looks like a dating or gambling context.
A starting point for free, anonymous or screen-based pictures: Unsplash's [customer-support search page](https://unsplash.com/s/photos/customer-support). **I did not verify any individual image on it** (licence page, model release or logos); that is done when the CEO chooses the option above, and I then assemble 10 to 15 candidates with their licence details for his approval.

## 5. What I need from the CEO
Which option (A, B, C or a mix); whether he is willing to be photographed and whether the CTO's portrait is wanted (and any operators who would consent); approval to **request a quote** from a paid stock agency or a photographer (no purchase); who owns the licence log.
