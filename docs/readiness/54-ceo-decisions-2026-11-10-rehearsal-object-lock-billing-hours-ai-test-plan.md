## 6. Billing: all three models, with verified hours
**Supported today (built, in the release):** hourly (scheduled hours); **hourly plus tickets above an included volume**; **per ticket**; **per moderated item**; **per message**; combinations through per-client settings (model, prices, included volume, VAT, payment terms), so **a different agreement is a setting, not a code change**.
**Not yet supported, requested 10 Nov: invoicing on actual, verified working hours.** Today hours come from **scheduled shifts net of absences**. Proposed design (not built; I recommend building it after the release unless the first client is on an hourly model):
- **Attendance record** per operator and shift: start and end taken from the operator's availability status (online and offline events already exist), **not** from a typed claim.
- **Timesheet per period:** the supervisor reviews and **approves** each operator's hours; adjustments (late start, outage, break) are entered with a reason; the invoice uses **approved hours**.
- **Absences:** an absence removes the scheduled hours; a **replacement** operator's hours are billed as the client's shift hours (one set of hours per covered shift, no double billing) and paid to whoever worked them.
- **Overtime:** hours beyond the scheduled shift count only if a supervisor approves them **and** the agreement allows them; a rate multiplier is a per-client setting.
- **Audit:** every adjustment records who, when and why; approved timesheets lock the period.
- **Effort:** about 1 to 1.5 weeks including tests (estimate). It changes the billing and earnings paths, so it needs your written approval and careful testing; it should not delay the release.
**Eligible ticket, definition proposed for each agreement (what the code does today in bold):** a ticket is a customer **conversation** counted **once**, in the period when it was **first resolved** (**the first close time is stored and a reopen never clears it**, so a reopened conversation is not billed twice); **at least one operator reply** is required (no replies, no charge: spam and duplicates are not billed); a conversation **still unresolved** at the end of the period is **not** billed until it is resolved, then in that period; an **escalated** conversation counts once, when it is finally resolved, by whoever resolves it; a **new customer message after a resolution** that the platform opens as a **new** conversation is a **new** ticket (the contract should say whether it is). The reply threshold, the treatment of escalations and the "new conversation after resolution" rule are **today fixed in code**; making them per-client settings is small (a few days) and I propose it with the verified-hours work.
**For clients working in their own helpdesk (Option A):** hours come from the platform's attendance (once built), ticket counts from the client's own reports entered per period (`ExternalTicketCount`, audited, locked once invoiced).
